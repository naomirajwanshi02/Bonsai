# Bonsai — research notes for the AR game

Collected 2026-11-10. These notes come from hobby guides, club sites and encyclopedia
entries. They disagree on details (timing, ratios), so the game uses the parts that most
sources agree on. Full links are in the Sources section at the end.

## 1. What a bonsai is
- A tree kept small in a shallow pot and shaped to look like a full-grown old tree in
  nature. The pot is part of the design.
- History: Chinese *penjing* (Tang-dynasty tomb fresco, after 684 CE) → Japan. The
  *Saigyō Monogatari Emaki* scroll (1195, Kamakura period) is the earliest Japanese picture
  of potted dwarf trees. Buddhist monks kept bonsai as a quiet, meditative practice.
- Size classes: **mame** under ~10 cm, **shohin** ~25 cm, larger trees need two people to carry.

## 2. Design rules (what makes a "good" bonsai)
Sources call these guidelines, not laws, but judges use them.
| Part | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Front | Every tree has one best viewing side: widest nebari, most taper, no branch pointing at the viewer. The back should look worse. |
| Trunk | Thick at the base, narrowing toward the top (**taper**). Reverse taper is the one rule people call unbreakable. The curve between roots and first branch is *tachiagari*. |
| Nebari | Surface roots flaring out from the base, wider than the trunk. Roots must not cross each other. |
| Branches | Start about **1/3 up** the trunk. Get thinner and shorter toward the top. Alternate left, right and back. No two branches at the same height (**bar branch**). No branch crosses the trunk from the front. |
| Silhouette | Roughly a **triangle**, but an uneven one (asymmetry). |
| Apex | Leans slightly toward the viewer. |
| Ramification | Branches split into finer and finer twigs toward their tips. |
| Proportion | Looks like a big tree: no thin trunk with thick branches, leaves small for the tree's size. |
| Visual age | Rough dark bark, deadwood, mass → feeling of age (*wabi / sabi*). |
| Pot | Small relative to the tree. Only tree, soil, moss and maybe natural rock inside. |

## 3. Styles (by trunk angle first)
| Style | Japanese | How to recognise it |
|---|---|---|
| Formal upright | Chokkan | Straight trunk, apex directly above base, triangle silhouette |
| Informal upright | Moyōgi | Curvy trunk, but apex still above the base |
| Slanting | Shakan | Trunk leaves soil at an angle, apex off to one side |
| Semi-cascade | Han-kengai | Tip drops to the pot rim or just below |
| Cascade | Kengai | Tip falls below the bottom of the pot |
| Literati | Bunjingi | Tall thin bendy trunk, very few branches near the top |
| Broom | Hōkidachi | Straight trunk, round fan of fine branches (deciduous, e.g. elm) |
| Windswept | Fukinagashi | Everything blown to one side |
| Root-over-rock / on rock | Sekijōju / Ishitsuki | Roots grip a stone |
| Twin / multi trunk | Sōkan / Kabudachi | Several trunks from one root, odd numbers |
| Forest | Yose-ue | Many trees, one species, shallow tray |

## 4. Techniques (the verbs of the game)
| Technique | What it does | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| Structural pruning | Remove whole branches to set the design | Permanent — do it gradually over seasons; late winter |
| Cut back to two | Leave 2 shoots at each fork | Stops ugly swelling; makes the branch split (ramification) |
| Pinching | Nip soft new tips | Light and selective; removes apical dominance so side buds grow |
| Back-budding | Hard prune → buds appear further back on the branch | Ficus, azalea respond well; pines don't |
| Wiring | Coil copper/aluminium wire on a branch, then bend it | Wire at ~45°; remove before it **bites** into bark; branches get brittle once hardened |
| Defoliation | Strip leaves of a strong deciduous tree | Early summer → smaller second leaves; never on a weak or just-repotted tree |
| Repotting / root pruning | New soil, trim roots | Early spring before buds open; never heavy root work + heavy top work in one season |
| Jin / Shari | Strip bark from a branch (jin) or trunk strip (shari), bleach with lime sulfur | Shows age and struggle; looks right on conifers (juniper, pine), fake on deciduous |
| Watering | Water when the top dries | Bonsai hate "wet feet"; **akadama soil turns lighter when dry** |
| Soil | Akadama + pumice + lava | Conifer ~1:1:1, deciduous ~2:1:1 |

## 5. Seasonal calendar (temperate trees, simplified)
| Season | Good jobs | Bad ideas |
|---|---|---|
| Late winter | Structural pruning, wiring bare branches | — |
| Early spring | Repot + root prune | Heavy pruning in same season |
| Late spring | Pinch new growth, wire young shoots | Repotting once buds have opened |
| Early summer | Defoliate strong deciduous trees, cut back | Defoliating weak trees |
| Summer | Clip-and-grow, watering often | Letting soil stay dry |
| Autumn | Let growth harden, enjoy colour | New hard pruning |

## 6. Species for the game
| Species | Type | Why it's good in a game |
|---|---|---|
| Juniper | Conifer, outdoor | Deadwood (jin/shari), dramatic styles, wiring |
| Japanese maple | Deciduous | Autumn colour, defoliation, ramification |
| Chinese elm | Deciduous | Broom style, forgiving |
| Ficus | Tropical, indoor | Beginner tree, back-buds after hard pruning, aerial roots |
| Japanese black pine | Conifer | "Cut to two buds" candle work (advanced) |

## 7. Display (tokonoma)
- Up to **three** things: the tree, an accent (*kusamono* plant, stone or figure), and
  optionally a hanging scroll.
- The tree is always the star. Accent and scroll echo the **season**.
- Stand colour ≠ pot colour. Formality: *shin* (formal) / *gyō* (semi) / *sō* (casual) —
  a literati tree suits a casual display.

## 8. Existing bonsai games (what's already out there)
- *Bonsai Barber* (Wii, 2009) — pruning as a haircut game.
- *Tree.Bonsai*, *Bonsai Life*, *Bonsai* (Steam) — grow/prune/repot sims; one review calls
  the 2016 *Bonsai* a "grindy clicker".
- Jam prototypes (*Bonsai Simulator*, *Speedy Bonsai*, *Branch Out*) — the *Bonsai Simulator*
  developer said pruning and wiring did not make it in; one *Speedy Bonsai* player said cut
  targeting felt imprecise.
- **Gap:** almost none teach the real design rules or score against them, and none we
  found has good wiring. That gap is where this game can stand out.
